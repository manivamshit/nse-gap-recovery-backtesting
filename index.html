01 / Research question

A simple trade. A difficult truth.

Could a negative opening gap predict an intraday bounce — and could that become a repeatable trading edge?

The signal

Stock opens 5–7% lower

Compare the official opening price with the previous closing price. Only the specified gap range qualifies.

The hypothetical trade

Buy open → sell close

No stop-loss or target. Subtract an illustrative 0.20 percentage points for round-trip costs.

The test

Does it work later?

Compare results across dates, remove influential sessions as stress tests, and evaluate a chronological validation period.

Why this matters: A high percentage of winning stock trades can come from one extraordinary market day. That is not the same as a strategy that wins consistently across days.

04 / Methodology

Transparent enough to challenge.

What was calculated, how the samples were split, and what the study cannot establish.

01 / PREPARE

Validate daily prices

Read NSE daily OHLC and volume, remove invalid price rows, and check symbol-date duplicates.

02 / DETECT

Classify opening gaps

Group negative gaps into 1–<3%, 3–<5%, 5–<7%, and 7–<10%.

03 / BACKTEST

Calculate net returns

Model buying at open and selling at close; also compare the opposite short direction.

04 / CHALLENGE

Validate and stress-test

Chronological 70/30 split, March 2 sensitivity, best-five-day removal, and day-level returns.

Calculation definitions

Opening gap

(Open / Previous close − 1) × 100

Hypothetical net long return

(Close / Open − 1) × 100 − 0.20

Trade win rate

Number of net-positive trades / all trades

Equal-day return

Mean of daily equal-weight signal-basket returns

Important limitations

Not verified fills: Daily OHLC does not prove simultaneous open-price execution, especially when selecting on the final opening price or market breadth.

Costs are illustrative: 0.20% is not a broker-specific fee, slippage, tax, or short-sale cost model.

Corporate actions: Large apparent gaps may reflect unadjusted splits or other actions; flagged events require verification.

Universe and sample: A present-day constituent list may introduce survivorship bias. The sample covers roughly one year, not multiple market cycles.

Statistical selection: Many overlapping rule combinations were explored, and validation samples are sometimes small.

Questions readers often ask

Does a 73.77% win rate mean I would win 74 out of 100 trades?

No. That was a historical aggregate strongly affected by one day containing 94 signals. Without that date the win rate fell to 53.93%.

Why does the average trade return differ from the average daily return?

A trade-level average gives every stock signal equal weight, so crowded days count more. A daily average first averages signals within each day, then gives each signal day equal weight.

Why not just remove 2 March permanently?

It is useful to measure how much the conclusion depends on that date, but excluding a real market day after seeing its outcome is not a valid executable trading rule.

Is this dashboard a live screener or a brokerage recommendation?

No. It is a static presentation of historical research summaries, with interactive ways to compare scenarios. It does not connect to a live exchange or place orders.

05 / The verdict

The discovery wasn't a magic rule.
It was a better way to test one.

No consistently validated 70% winning strategy was established.

The 5–7% gap-down recovery rule showed a 73.77% historical net win rate, but 51.4% of its trades occurred on 2 March 2026. Excluding that session reduced the rate to 53.93%. In the later validation period, the rule won 50.00% of its 26 trades, and its equal-day average was −0.52%. A broader rule search found 29 combinations above 70% in training, but none passed the predefined validation screen.

The practical lesson: Check whether a backtest survives influential dates, changing market regimes, trading-day weighting, and truly independent future data before calling it an edge.

Research transparency / Important notice

Methodology, limitations & disclaimer

How this study was conducted

Historical daily OHLC data were screened for opening gaps, calculated as (open / previous close − 1) × 100. The hypothetical strategy enters at the reported opening price and exits at the same-day closing price, subtracting an illustrative 0.20 percentage-point round-trip cost. Outcomes are evaluated by trade-level win rate, mean net return, and equal-weight signal-day returns. Rules were explored on an earlier 70% chronological training period and checked on a later 30% validation period. Sensitivity tests excluded the unusually crowded 2 March 2026 session and the five strongest remaining days.

What the results cannot establish

Daily OHLC bars do not verify achievable opening fills, actual liquidity, slippage, fees, taxes, or short-sale availability. Unverified corporate-action adjustments may distort apparent gaps. A present-day constituent universe may create survivorship bias. The sample spans approximately one year; multiple overlapping rule tests and small validation samples can exaggerate apparent patterns. Removing exceptional days after observing their outcomes is a diagnostic stress test, not a prospective trading rule. Historical performance does not establish future returns.
